2 · Who’s booking, and what they need to know first

Three audiences with different questions. The page order follows the first-timer, because returning drivers can skip ahead and first-timers can’t.

The first-timer

Owns a fun car, has never driven a track.

  • Is this safe and am I good enough?
  • What does it really cost, all in?
  • What do I bring, and do I need a helmet?

The returning student

Has done 2–5 events, still instructed.

  • Which group am I in now?
  • When does early-bird end?
  • Book in under two minutes

The solo driver

Signed off to drive without an instructor.

  • Am I still solo-approved at this track?
  • Get the solo discount without calling
  • Car number and shared-car setup

These are assumptions based on the content. A real engagement starts by checking them: 5–6 calls with recent customers, plus TDE staff on what people phone about most.

3 · Key decisions and the trade-offs behind them

Two doors: new or returning

Step 1 starts with a tab, not a sentence. New drivers describe their experience in plain words and TDE places them. Returning drivers pick the group they already know, and only that event’s set is shown: Purple, Orange and Pink on 1-day events; Green → Red on 2-day schools. TDE still confirms every placement.

Price on every option

Showing $365 next to “New driver” may feel worse than “from $275.” It isn’t: an honest number beats a surprise at checkout, and the early-bird line gives a reason to book now.

Questions before the button

Schedule, checklist and solo rules sit beside the form so nobody leaves the page to find them. Long policy text stays on its own page, summarized in two lines here.

Kill the Google Doc

An events list on the site is one source of truth, indexable by search, and keeps people in the funnel. Needs the event records cleaned up in WooCommerce first.

Keep the brand, fix the voice

Same logo, deep purple carbon fiber, red redline and white — plus the tachometer motif from their artwork. What changes is how it’s used: red only for actions and urgency, purple for the frame, plenty of white for reading. The run-group swatches are kept brighter than the brand purple so “Purple group” never reads as decoration.

Mobile first for the card

Registrations likely spike on phones the night registration opens. One column, 44 px targets, numeric keypad for car number, total always visible.

4 · How I’d validate it

Before building

  • Pull 12 months of orders: how many picked a group staff later changed?
  • Log a week of phone calls by topic
  • Five moderated tests: “book the Dec 12 event as a first-timer”

What to measure

  • Event page → completed booking rate
  • Time to book for returning drivers
  • Calls about run groups and pricing
  • Bookings needing a group correction

Ship order

  • Week 1: clean the dropdowns, price per option, fix dead links — no redesign needed
  • Then: new event template
  • Then: homepage events list, retire the Google Doc
